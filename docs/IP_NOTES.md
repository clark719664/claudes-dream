# Originality notes

This game takes structural inspiration from two well-known mobile titles: a
shape-placement puzzle, and a travel-and-build progression loop. Those loops are
**game mechanics**, and mechanics are not what copyright or trademark protect.
What is protected is **expression** — names, characters, artwork, layouts and
trade dress — and none of that is borrowed here.

This is a record of what was deliberately avoided, so it stays avoided as the
game grows. **It is not legal advice, and nobody involved in writing it is a
lawyer.** Before any commercial release, have an IP solicitor review the build,
the store listing and the marketing.

## Not used anywhere in this project

**Names and words.** No existing game's title, sub-title or tagline appears in
the code, the UI, the repository, the store metadata or the documentation —
including in comments, variable names and test fixtures. The brief given to any
art tool must not name one either, because an artist told to match a named game
will reproduce its trade dress.

**Characters and mascots.** No mascot of any kind is borrowed. Album stickers
are original creatures, objects and motifs written for this game.

**Board design and trade dress.** The progression track is a **ring of light
nodes** — deliberately not a square board with corner squares, not a perimeter
of property spaces, and with no coloured property bands. There is no jail, no
"go to jail", no chance or community-chest equivalent by any name, no title
deeds, no houses or hotels, no rent charged between players, and no auction.

**Currency and naming.** Currencies are **Charges**, **Lumens**, **Prism
Shards** and **Dust**. Structures are **Beacons**. Locations are **Sectors**,
named Shoal Light, The Foundry and Nullspace. Track spaces are Origin, Cache,
Vault, Surge, Relay, Signal, Prism and Drain. All original, and all chosen to
fit this game's own prism-and-light theme rather than to echo anything.

**Visual identity.** The palette, tile shapes, typography and UI are this
project's own. Nothing is traced, sampled or colour-matched from another game's
screenshots.

## What is shared, and why that is fine

- **Dropping polyominoes onto a grid to complete lines** — a mechanic, and one
  with prior art going back to the 1980s.
- **Spending a currency to move around a track and collecting on where you
  land** — a mechanic, and one with prior art going back roughly forever.
- **Collectible sticker sets with duplicates and trading** — a mechanic, and
  one older than video games.

Mechanics being unprotected is the general position in US and UK law, but it is
a general position, not a guarantee about any particular case.

## Rules to keep to

1. Never name another game in the product, the repository, the store listing or
   any brief sent to a person or a tool.
2. Never ask for art "in the style of" a named game. Describe the requirement —
   size, palette, silhouette, mood — as `docs/GEMINI_ART_BRIEF.md` does.
3. Keep the track a ring. The moment it becomes a square with corner spaces and
   coloured bands, it starts to resemble specific trade dress.
4. If a feature only makes sense as a reference to another game, cut it.
5. Get a solicitor's review before any commercial release.
