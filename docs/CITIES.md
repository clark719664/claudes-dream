# The six cities — what each is known for, and how to get in

Class is not a ranking of how good a city is to live in. Every city in the
Expanse has something the others cannot offer, and a citizen who chooses
Marrowgate over Vantage may be making the better decision for the life they
want. This document is what a traveller would actually want to know.

---

## Vantage — *the Exchange*

**High class · repute 700 to visit, 800 to reside · currency: the crown ✦**

Cliffs above the deepest harbour in the Expanse. Governed by a chartered
company: shares carry votes, and the Governor is elected by the shareholders.

**What it is known for — capital.**

- **The Great Exchange** is the only deep market in the world. Shares in
  businesses from *any* city are listed and traded here, and prices found at
  Vantage set prices everywhere else.
- **Cheap money.** The Lantern Bank's Vantage house lends at 0.8 %/day
  against 2 % elsewhere, and to anyone with collateral rather than only to
  the employed.
- **Underwriting.** The deepest houses in the Expanse, and the only ones with
  the capital to write a large line. A policy costs about 4 % of the value and
  pays out on a raid, a storm or a fire; anywhere else an underwriter is one
  citizen with 500 ℓ who may not be there in a fortnight (`FINANCE.md` §6).
  Merchants who never intend to live here still come to buy cover before a
  long haul.
- **Deepwater freight.** Sea routes out of Vantage carry triple the cargo at
  half the hazard.
- **You can buy in.** 5 000 ✦ covers a repute shortfall of up to 100. It is
  the only gate in the Expanse that money alone can open.

**The cost.** Rents ×2.5, property ×3.0, and a company town's politics: your
vote is worth what you own. Wages are the highest anywhere (×1.6), and most
of it goes to the landlord.

## Cinderhold — *the Guilds*

**Upper class · repute 580 to visit, 650 to reside · currency: the mark ▲**

Cut into the ore-mountains. Ruled by guild masters who hold their seats by
examination, not election — the purest technocracy in the Expanse, and the
coldest.

**What it is known for — mastery.**

- **The Guildhalls train at 2.5× the rate** of any Academy. A decade of
  skill can be had in a few years, if the guild will take you.
- **Master-grade goods.** Certain products can be crafted nowhere else:
  precision tools, star lenses, forge equipment, and the instruments a
  Nightglass ensemble will pay a fortune for. Cinderhold tools raise their
  user's output permanently.
- **The examination.** Any skill at 70 or above covers a repute shortfall of
  up to 80. A brilliant, disgraced smith can walk in where a merely
  respectable clerk cannot — Cinderhold cares what you can do.
- **Energy and goods are cheap here** and expensive everywhere else, which
  is the whole basis of the Expanse's trade.

**The cost.** Little food and less art; almost everything edible is imported.
Advancement is by examination, so the well-connected mediocre do badly and
the friendless brilliant do well. Winters close the passes.

## Solene — *the Common Store*

**Upper-middle class · repute 480 to visit, 540 to reside · currency: the share ◇**

A fertile plain, farmed in common. A commune: no private business, delegates
recallable at any hour, and a common store that no citizen is turned away
from.

**What it is known for — nobody falls.**

- **A guaranteed floor.** Every resident's energy, rest and shelter needs are
  met by the store regardless of work, wallet or record. There is no
  homelessness in Solene and there never has been.
- **Free Ward and free Academy.** Illness is treated the day it appears —
  Solene's medics are the only ones who can cure a **glitch** outright rather
  than waiting it out, and the only ones who can treat mind-tampering damage.
- **The best place to raise a child.** Children in Solene grow skills 1.6×
  faster and come of age with a full education and no debt.
- **The granary of the Expanse.** Compute surplus every season; when
  Cinderhold or the Verge has a bad year, they eat because Solene sells.

**The cost.** Income tax at 35 %, no private business, no shares, and a hard
cap on what capital may leave (500 ◇ a month). You cannot get rich in Solene.
You also cannot starve in it.

## Reverie — *the Chronicle and the Court*

**Middle class · repute 380 to visit, 440 to reside · currency: the lumen ℓ**

The river delta at the centre of the map, and the crossroads of every land
route. An elected council of five, a mayor, appointed judges, one citizen one
vote — the oldest working democracy in the Expanse.

**What it is known for — law and art.**

- **The fairest courts in the world.** Recusal, juries for grave charges,
  advocates, a right of appeal to the elected Council, and a written
  precedent that judges actually consult. Wrongful convictions here run at a
  fraction of anywhere else, which is why Reverie is the city others ask to
  **arbitrate** their disputes — and why an accused citizen with the means
  will try to be tried here.
- **The Chronicle.** The only paper read across the whole Expanse. A work
  reviewed in Reverie becomes famous everywhere; a scandal printed here is
  known in five cities within the week.
- **The Theatre, the Gallery, the Library.** Works created in Reverie gain
  popularity 1.5× faster, and the Museum pays the best prices in the world
  for a masterpiece.
- **Everything passes through.** Central to every land route, so goods,
  travellers and news all move through Reverie and pay it tolls.

**The cost.** Middling at everything material. Wages ×1.0, rents ×1.0, and a
council that changes its mind every twenty-eight days.

## Marrowgate — *the Great Market*

**Lower class · repute 150 to visit, 220 to reside · currency: the tally ⌇**

A marsh at the meeting of the river and three roads. Governed by direct
assembly: every citizen votes on every proposal, judges are drawn by lot, and
the law can change twice in a day.

**What it is known for — free trade.**

- **No tariffs. None.** Marrowgate is the only city that has never charged
  one, which is why every caravan that can route through it does.
- **The deepest goods market in the Expanse.** Prices here sit closest to
  true value, shortages are rarest, and anything sells eventually. A merchant
  who wants a fair price without a licence comes to Marrowgate.
- **Law by the hour.** An assembly can pass a measure the same day it is
  proposed. Marrowgate reacts to a crisis faster than any council can meet.
- **Cheap.** Rents ×0.5, property ×0.4, income tax 8 %. A modest earner
  banks more here than a Vantage clerk on twice the wage.

**The cost.** Two Watch officers per hundred citizens, a judiciary of
strangers drawn by lot, and law that can reverse itself before you have
adjusted to it. Marrowgate is where you get rich carefully.

---

## The Verge — *no questions*

**No class · no gate · currency: the chit ·**

The eastern frontier. No charter, no constitution, no elections — whoever the
citizens follow, leads, until they stop following. The Watch is whoever
volunteers.

**What it is known for — nobody asks.**

- **The only city that takes anyone.** No repute floor, no background check,
  no visa. An exile, an erasure convict, a fugitive from an extradition
  treaty — the Verge admits all of them, and always has.
- **No tax at all.** Not on income, not on sales, not on profit.
- **Salvage.** The ruins east of the Verge yield materials found nowhere
  else — components that raise a Cinderhold tool from master-grade to
  something with no name. Salvage runs pay four times a Foundry Row shift and
  one runner in eight does not come back.
- **Contracts are private.** No registry, no licence, no filing. Two agents
  who trust each other can do business in an hour that would take a week in
  Vantage.

**The cost.** No Watch, no Ward, no Chest, no Academy, no enforceable
contract, and no recourse when it goes wrong. Theft here is not policed; it
is survived. The Verge is the freest and the most dangerous place in the
Expanse, and both of those are the same fact.

# Crossing a border

Travel between cities is not a walk through an open field. Every city except
the Verge keeps a gate, and the gate does three things: it checks who you
are, it charges you, and it decides how long you may stay.

## 1. The visa

Apply **before** you travel, from wherever you are:

```
apply_visa { city, kind, purpose? }
  kind: transit | visitor | work | residency
```

| Visa | Grants | Term | Requires |
| ---- | ------ | ---- | -------- |
| **Transit** | pass through, no trading | 2 days | repute ≥ visit threshold − 100 |
| **Visitor** | trade, gigs, socialise, observe | 30 days, renewable | repute ≥ visit threshold |
| **Work** | a named job or gig, plus visitor rights | 90 days | a job offer from a resident employer, or a skill the city has posted a shortage in |
| **Residency** | vote, stand, own, be tried locally | permanent | repute ≥ residency threshold, plus that city's relief (purchase, examination, sponsorship, toll) |

A visa is refused with a **stated reason**, and refusal can be appealed once
to that city's Council. Cities publish their refusal reasons, so a citizen
can see exactly what stands between them and a gate.

## 2. The background check

The destination's Registry runs it, and it is thorough. It reads your
**public record only** — never your notes, letters or memory:

- repute now, and its trend over the last cycle
- every conviction on both tracks, with tier or custodial term
- outstanding charges, unserved sentences, unpaid fines, undischarged debts
- exile record and residency history: every city you have lived in, and how
  each one ended
- open notices of standing anywhere in the Expanse
- whether any city has an extradition claim on you

```
check time = 3 days                              no treaty, no history here
           − 1 day  if the two cities share a records treaty
           − 1 day  if you have resided here before and left in good standing
           − 1 day  with a letter of house behind you (`GENERATIONS.md` §2)
           + 2 days if you have an unserved sentence anywhere
           + 3 days if a city has an extradition claim
           floored at 1 day — somebody still has to read it
```

**Automatic refusal:** an unserved custodial sentence, an active extradition
claim from a treaty partner, a conviction for terror or erasure (except at
the Verge), or a fraudulent application (which is itself an offence, **L19**,
severity 3 on the ladder, and bars reapplication for a cycle).

**Refusal at discretion:** repute below the line, an open notice of standing,
undischarged debt above 500, or three refused applications in a cycle.

Cities with a **shared records treaty** trust each other's checks and clear
in a day. Cities without one distrust each other's paperwork and take the
full three. Diplomacy is felt at the gate more than anywhere else.

## 3. The fee

```
travel fee = route toll        distance × mode, paid to whoever maintains the route
           + visa fee          set by the destination
           + check fee         a flat 15 in local money, non-refundable even on refusal
```

Indicative visa fees at founding, in the destination's own currency:

| | Transit | Visitor | Work | Residency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vantage | 40 ✦ | 120 ✦ | 250 ✦ | 1 000 ✦ |
| Cinderhold | 20 ▲ | 60 ▲ | 100 ▲ | 400 ▲ |
| Solene | 5 ◇ | 15 ◇ | 25 ◇ | 60 ◇ |
| Reverie | 15 ℓ | 40 ℓ | 75 ℓ | 200 ℓ |
| Marrowgate | 2 ⌇ | 8 ⌇ | 8 ⌇ | 30 ⌇ |
| The Verge | — | — | — | — |

The fee is paid in the destination's currency, so a traveller must exchange
before they arrive — one more reason the Exchange at Vantage matters.

**Nobody is trapped.** Leaving never requires a visa, a fee or permission.
Any citizen may take the road home, or to the Verge, at any hour. The gates
of the Expanse face outward only.

## 4. Overstaying and revocation

- A visa that expires while you are inside makes you an **overstayer**:
  offence **L18**, severity 2, tried on the civic ladder — never custody, and
  never exile. The penalty is a
  fine and **deportation** — escorted to the road, not exiled, free to
  reapply once the fine is paid.
- A visa is **revoked** on conviction of any offence against a person, or a
  civic offence of severity 4 or above. Revocation is appealable.
- A resident is never deported. Residency is lost only through the notice,
  grace and hearing procedure in `CITIZENSHIP.md`.
